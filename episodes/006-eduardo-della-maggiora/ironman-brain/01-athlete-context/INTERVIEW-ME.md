# Athlete Context Interview

## Instructions for the Interviewer

Ask one question at a time. Follow the athlete's language, probe concrete examples,
and distinguish current facts from old history or aspirations. Offer a short recap
after each section and ask the athlete to correct it before updating
[[ATHLETE-PROFILE]]. Do not turn the interview into a diagnosis or a generic intake
form.

## 1. The Athlete and the Why

- What are you training for, and why does it matter now?
- If the next year goes well, what changes beyond a finish time?
- What part of endurance sport gives you energy? What part quietly takes it away?
- Which identity matters most when sport conflicts with family, work, or health?
- What would make a technically successful season feel like a personal failure?

## 2. Race and Performance History

- Which races, distances, and environments best represent you?
- Where do you consistently gain or lose time?
- What have your best performances had in common during the preceding eight weeks?
- What patterns preceded your worst races, injuries, or withdrawals?
- Which personal records are comparable, and which came from unusual courses,
  devices, conditions, or data errors?

## 3. Training Reality

- Describe a normal week as it actually happens.
- Which sessions are protected, movable, or frequently sacrificed?
- What training do you enjoy enough to sustain for years?
- Where do intensity, volume, technique, and strength currently appear?
- How do you decide to progress, maintain, reduce, or stop a session?

## 4. Recovery and Readiness

- What does good recovery feel like before a device tells you anything?
- Which sleep, HRV, resting heart rate, soreness, mood, or motivation signals have
  proved meaningful for you?
- Which scores make you anxious or cause you to overreact?
- How do travel, alcohol, illness, heat, altitude, and work stress change your
  recovery?
- What is your current minimum effective recovery routine?

## 5. Fueling and Body Composition

- What do you eat on ordinary days, long sessions, race mornings, and during races?
- What have you learned about carbohydrates, hydration, sodium, caffeine, protein,
  and gastrointestinal tolerance?
- Are weight or body-composition goals helping performance, or competing with
  health and recovery?
- Which nutrition rules are evidence from your own experience, and which are beliefs
  inherited from others?
- What should the agent never recommend about food or weight?

## 6. Health, Injury, and Biomarkers

- Which diagnoses, injuries, surgeries, medications, supplements, allergies, or
  family-history risks are relevant to training?
- What recurring pain or symptoms tend to be normalized?
- Which labs or clinical measures are actively followed, and by whom?
- What would require an immediate coach or clinician conversation?
- Which health information may the agent use, and which should remain outside the
  training system?

## 7. Life Load

- What does a demanding work week look like?
- Which family, caregiving, travel, sleep, and social commitments are non-negotiable?
- When has training made the rest of life better? When has it made life smaller?
- How much scheduling flexibility exists on weekdays and weekends?
- What is the smallest viable training week during disruption?

## 8. Decision Style

- Do you respond better to a plan, a range, an experiment, or a direct challenge?
- When should the agent disagree with you?
- Which decisions may it suggest, and which always require a coach or clinician?
- What evidence earns your trust?
- How should it communicate when the data are incomplete or contradictory?

## 9. The First Useful Question

- What decision are you facing this week that this system should help clarify?
- What data and context would genuinely change that decision?
- What would a cautious, useful answer look like?
