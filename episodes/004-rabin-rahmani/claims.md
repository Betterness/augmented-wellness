# Claims and evidence boundaries

This artifact is designed for context collection and visit preparation. Its value does not depend on making diagnostic claims.

## Supported uses

- Organizing a symptom and care timeline.
- Keeping tests and procedures tied to their dates and source documents.
- Preparing questions for a qualified clinician.
- Comparing self-reported patterns across time without claiming causation.
- Using explicitly permissioned Betterness data to reduce repetitive data entry.

## Language standard

Use:

- "The report states..."
- "I noticed..."
- "This pattern may be worth discussing..."
- "The available information does not establish why this happened."
- "A qualified clinician can interpret this in medical context."

Avoid:

- "This proves..."
- "You have..."
- "This rules out..."
- "The best treatment is..."
- "Your microbiome caused..."
- "A normal range means nothing is wrong."

## Episode-specific boundaries

- AI-assisted polyp detection may support clinicians during colonoscopy; the episode does not establish that it replaces physician review or guarantees cancer prevention.
- Microbiome research is relevant to many digestive conditions, but a consumer microbiome result should not be treated as a stand-alone diagnosis or treatment plan.
- GLP-1 medications have established clinical uses and evolving evidence. Individual benefits, side effects, contraindications, and long-term questions belong in clinician-led care.
- IBS, IBD, and EPI are distinct clinical concepts. A symptom portfolio must not collapse them into one diagnosis.
- Laboratory reference ranges require context, but an AI should not invent a personalized "normal" range without validated clinical authority.
